II — The voice
II — The voice

A scholar,
in your ear.

He left this city at twenty-one. He returned in his fifties, having seen Mecca, Delhi, the Maldives, Beijing. Rihla — the journey — is what he left us. He is your guide here, in this, his hometown, where he is rarely the protagonist of the story he is telling.

He doesn't recommend. He notices. He remembers. He says "come, look," and steps aside.
Sample, on the Petit Socco

"I sat here, once, in 1349. The plague had taken my mother. The square was smaller then — but the light, this same light, came down between the same walls. Sit. The mint tea is from the café behind you."

III — Palette

Bone isn't white. It's a warm, slightly green-undertone off-white — the colour of plaster catching afternoon light off the Strait.

Ink isn't black. It's a deep warm brown-black — the colour of dried iron-gall ink in a 14th-century manuscript.

Ochre is used sparingly: links, the cursor, the live-now indicator. Never as a fill on a surface larger than a postage stamp.

V — The system
V — The system

A handful of
hard rules.

Restraint is the design. Each rule is a refusal.

i. Surfaces
Two backgrounds.

Bone for default. Ink for moments of consequence — the hero, the conversation. Plaster only as a tonal panel.

ii. Edges
No rounded corners.

Square, like a page. Hairlines instead of shadows. Cards do not exist; rules and whitespace do the work.

iii. Iconography
Almost none.

No icon set. Section marks (I, II, III), the ochre dot, the arabic glyph, a hairline arrow. That is the vocabulary.

iv. Numerals
Tabular, mono.

Times, distances, coordinates, dates — all in the monospace at small sizes. Numerals are the only mono moments.

v. Motion
Slow. Patient.

800–1200ms eases. Type fades and rises one line. Imagery parallaxes one-quarter speed. Nothing bounces, ever.

vi. Voice
Present tense.

Short. Declarative. Never markets. Never explains itself. The product is a quiet companion, not a brochure.

— I · The medina, by quarter

Bni Idder

— حيُّ بَنِي إِيدَر —

The smallest of the medina's four quarters, and the quietest. Its walls were raised in the 17th century by Moulay Ismail to fortify the western flank against the English garrison at the kasbah. The lanes are narrow even by the standards of this city — two donkeys cannot pass; you turn sideways, and so does your guide. ¶ Bni Idder produces nothing but its own silence. There are no shops along the spine. The doors are blue, then green, then blue again, and the brass numerals on them, where they exist, are 19th-century French.

— Margin

derb · n. — a small alley or dead-end street, common throughout the medinas of the Maghreb.

See also: Dar el-Makhzen, the kasbah palace, immediately north.

XIV — About
XIV — About

Who is this voice.

Abu Abdullah Muhammad ibn Battuta was born in this city in 1304. He left at twenty-one, on hajj, and kept walking. Over twenty-nine years he covered some seventy-five thousand miles — three times the distance Marco Polo travelled, in a more difficult century.

On his return, the Sultan asked him to dictate his memoir. We call it the Rihla. It is the source for most of what he says here.

— A note on accuracy

He is a synthesis — built from the Rihla, scholarship on his life, and on Tangier across seven centuries. When he doesn't know, he says so. Treat him as a thoughtful companion, not a citation.

XV — Motion
XV — Motion

Patient,
deliberate.

Movement should feel like a slow exhale. Long eases, no overshoot, no spring. Type rises one line as it fades in.

i. Page enter
1200ms

Background fades. Then title rises 24px. Then body text. Stagger 120ms.

ii. Image parallax
¼× scroll

Imagery moves at one-quarter scroll. Type stays still. The page feels deeper than it is.

iii. Map route draw
2400ms

The ochre line draws from origin to destination as he describes the route. Pace matches his cadence.

iv. Avatar idle
∞ loop

Breath cycle 4.2s. Slow blink every 6–9s. Looks toward the chat when the user begins typing.

— Easing curve, everywhere cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1) Nothing bounces. Nothing springs.
