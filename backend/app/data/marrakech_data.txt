Marrakech, Morocco: A Comprehensive Guide

--- HISTORY AND OVERVIEW ---
Marrakech, often called the "Red City" for its distinctive pink-red sandstone buildings, is one of Morocco's four imperial cities. Founded in 1062 by the Almoravid dynasty, it served as the capital of the Almoravid and Almohad empires and gave its name to the entire country in several European languages. Situated at the edge of the Sahara with the High Atlas Mountains as a backdrop, Marrakech is a city where ancient traditions meet vibrant modernity. Ibn Battuta himself passed through Morocco many times and would have known of Marrakech's rich heritage. Today it is Morocco's fourth-largest city and one of Africa's most visited destinations, welcoming over 3 million tourists annually.

--- JEMAA EL-FNA AND THE MEDINA ---
The heart of Marrakech is Jemaa el-Fna, a UNESCO-recognized square and one of the world's great public spaces. By day the square fills with orange juice vendors, henna artists, snake charmers and acrobats. As evening falls, hundreds of food stalls materialize from nowhere, filling the air with the smoke and aroma of grilled meats, harira soup, snails and fresh bread. The surrounding medina (old city), a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1985, is a labyrinth of narrow souks organized by trade: tanners, weavers, coppersmiths, spice merchants and leather workers all have their dedicated quarters. Getting deliberately lost in the medina is a rite of passage for visitors. The main souk street Souk Semmarine leads north from the square and branches into dozens of sub-souks. Best visited in the morning before the crowds arrive.

--- KOUTOUBIA MOSQUE AND ISLAMIC ARCHITECTURE ---
The Koutoubia Mosque, completed in 1158 under the Almohad Caliph Yaqub al-Mansur, is the largest mosque in Marrakech and one of the finest examples of Moorish architecture in the world. Its 77-meter minaret inspired the Giralda in Seville and the Hassan Tower in Rabat, all built by the same dynasty. The minaret is decorated with a different ornamental design on each of its four sides. Non-Muslims may not enter the mosque, but the surrounding gardens and exterior are open to all. The Ben Youssef Mosque and Madrasa, a 14th-century Islamic school, is open to visitors and contains some of the most intricate zellij tilework, carved stucco and cedarwood architecture in Morocco. The Saadian Tombs, discovered by chance in 1917, date from the late 16th century and contain the elaborate mausoleums of Sultan Ahmad al-Mansur and his family amid stunning Italianate marble and carved plasterwork.

--- PALACES AND ROYAL HERITAGE ---
The Bahia Palace, whose name means "brilliance," was built in the late 19th century for Si Moussa, grand vizier of Morocco. Its 8 hectares include lavish apartments, a large riad garden, painted cedar ceilings, zellige floors and carved plaster walls reflecting the finest craftsmanship of the era. It was intended to be the greatest palace of its time. The ruined El Badi Palace, commissioned by the Saadian Sultan Ahmad al-Mansur in 1578 to celebrate his victory over Portugal at the Battle of Three Kings, was once described as one of the finest palaces in the world. Portuguese prisoners were reportedly used in its construction. Today only the vast sunken courtyard, pavilion bases and subterranean storage vaults remain after the Alaouite Sultan Moulay Ismail stripped its treasures and marble to build his palace at Meknes in the 17th century. Storks nest on its walls in season.

--- GARDENS AND NATURAL BEAUTY ---
Marrakech is a city of extraordinary gardens. The Majorelle Garden, designed by French painter Jacques Majorelle and later owned and restored by fashion designer Yves Saint Laurent, is a vivid botanical garden featuring over 300 plant species from five continents, cobalt blue buildings and a cactus garden. It also houses the Berber Museum. The Agdal Gardens, a vast irrigated orchard and garden dating from the 12th century, extend 3 km south of the medina. In spring the almond and olive trees bloom and locals picnic under their shade. The Menara Gardens, also ancient, surround a large reflecting pool backed by the Atlas Mountains — one of the most iconic images of Marrakech. The palm grove (Palmeraie) on the city's outskirts provides shade for camel rides and cycling among thousands of date palms.

--- FOOD AND RESTAURANTS ---
Marrakech cuisine is a highlight of any visit. Traditional Moroccan dishes to seek out include pastilla (pigeon or seafood pie with almonds and cinnamon wrapped in thin warka pastry), mechoui (slow-roasted whole lamb carved to order), couscous with seven vegetables, and a dozen varieties of tagine. The spice market in the medina is the place to buy preserved lemons, argan oil, ras el hanout and saffron. Nomad restaurant near the Bahia Palace offers modern Moroccan cuisine with rooftop views. Le Jardin in the medina serves international food in a green courtyard garden. For street food, try the Djemaa el-Fna stalls for harira, msemen flatbread and fresh orange juice. Café de France on the main square is a classic spot for mint tea and people-watching. Upscale riads throughout the medina offer private evening dining experiences under the stars.

--- DAY TRIPS AND ATLAS MOUNTAINS ---
Marrakech is an ideal base for day trips. The High Atlas Mountains are only 60 km away and easily accessible. Ourika Valley, about 30 km south, offers Berber villages, cascading waterfalls and mountain treks. The village of Imlil at 1740 m elevation is the gateway to Mount Toubkal (4167 m), the highest peak in North Africa and a popular two-day trekking destination. Ouarzazate, "the door of the desert," lies 3 hours over the Tizi n'Tichka pass (2260 m) and is famous for its kasbahs, film studios (Lawrence of Arabia, Gladiator and Game of Thrones were filmed nearby), and the UNESCO-listed Aït Benhaddou fortified village. The Erg Chebbi sand dunes of Merzouga, while a full day's drive, offer classic Sahara camel-trekking and overnight desert camp experiences. The Cascades d'Ouzoud, Morocco's most spectacular waterfalls at 110 meters, are a 3-hour drive north and a perfect day trip combining swimming, hiking and Barbary macaque spotting.
