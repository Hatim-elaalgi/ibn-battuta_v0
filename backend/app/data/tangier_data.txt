Tangier, Morocco: A Comprehensive Guide

--- History ---
Tangier (Arabic: Tanjah) is a major city in northwestern Morocco on the Maghreb coast. It is located on the Strait of Gibraltar, where the Mediterranean Sea meets the Atlantic Ocean.
Founded by Carthaginian colonists in the early 5th century BC, likely on an earlier Berber settlement.
Throughout history, it has been a strategic gateway between Africa and Europe, controlled by Phoenicians, Romans, Vandals, Byzantines, Arabs, Portuguese, Spanish, and British at various times.
From 1923 to 1956, Tangier had "International Zone" status, making it a destination for many diplomats, spies, writers, and businessmen.
Returned to Moroccan sovereignty in 1956 following independence.

--- Geography & Climate ---
Located at the western entrance to the Strait of Gibraltar.
The climate is Mediterranean, with heavy rainfall than most parts of North Africa and mild summers.
Key areas include the Medina (old city), the Ville Nouvelle (new city), and the Malabata and Marshan districts.

--- Important Landmarks ---
1. The Kasbah: A fortified complex on the highest point of the city, offering panoramic views of the Strait of Gibraltar and Spain. Features include the Kasbah Museum (located in the former Sultan's palace, Dar el Makhzen).
2. Grand Socco (Place April 9, 1947): The official entrance to the Medina and a busy transport hub. It marks the divide between the old Medina and the new city.
3. Petit Socco: A smaller square inside the Medina, famous for its historic cafes where writers like Paul Bowles and William S. Burroughs once sat.
4. American Legation Museum: The only US National Historic Landmark located in a foreign country. It commemorates the diplomatic relations between the US and Morocco (the first country to recognize US independence). Be sure to see the "Paul Bowles Wing".
5. Hercules Caves (Grottes d'Hercule): Located 14km west of Tangier. Natural limestone caves with a sea opening shaped like the map of Africa. Myth says Hercules rested here before his 11th labor.
6. Cape Spartel: The northwesternmost point of Africa, where the Atlantic meets the Mediterranean. Features a historic lighthouse built in 1864.

--- Cultural Notes ---
Languages: Darija (Moroccan Arabic) is the main dialect. French and Spanish are widely spoken due to colonial history.
Tea Culture: Mint tea (Atay) is a symbol of hospitality, often served very sweet.
The "Tangier International Zone" era (1923-1956) influence is still visible in architecture and the multicultural vibe.
Famous for its literary history, attracting Beat Generation writers in the 1950s.

--- Tourist Attractions & Activities ---
- Wandering the Medina: A maze of narrow alleyways filled with shops selling rugs, leather goods, spices, and brassware.
- Strolling the Corniche: A modern beachfront promenade lined with cafes and restaurants.
- Café Hafa: An iconic cliffside cafe open since 1921, famous for its mint tea and views of the ocean.
- Perdicaris Park (Rmilat): A forest park overlooking the sea, great for hiking and picnics.
- Mnara (or Malabata) Lighthouse: view point on the east side of the bay.

--- Cuisine ---
Key dishes: Tagine (slow-cooked stew), Couscous (usually Fridays), Harira (soup), Grilled Sardines, and various seafood dishes due to coastal location.
Street Food: Kalinte (chickpea tart), Bocadillos (specifically Tangier-style sandwhiches).
